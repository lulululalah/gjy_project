## 论文可用英文结果段落

To evaluate the effect of feature removal on surface-mesh complexity, three representative aircraft models were meshed before and after simplification using identical OpenCASCADE controls: a relative linear deflection of 0.0005 and an angular deflection of 0.5 rad. Each meshing stage was repeated five times, and the median meshing time was recorded. All six STEP inputs passed the B-Rep validity check. Compared with the original models, simplification reduced the surface-triangle count by 3.35% for Cessna Citation 2, 35.59% for the 747-400, and 15.31% for the DC-10. The corresponding reductions in median meshing time were 21.54%, 45.84%, and 57.40%, respectively. Surface-mesh node counts also decreased for all three models.

The quality distributions remained comparable after simplification. The number of degenerate triangles did not increase in any model pair, while the 5th-percentile minimum angle and 95th-percentile aspect ratio improved for Cessna Citation 2 and the 747-400. The DC-10 showed a small reverse change in these two distributional statistics, so the results do not indicate uniform quality improvement. Bidirectional sampled surface comparisons further showed that the surfaces retained in the simplified models remained coincident with the original models at the sampled locations. The reverse comparison from the original model to the simplified model includes intentionally removed small features and should therefore not be interpreted as a global shell-error measure.
